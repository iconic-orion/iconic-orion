What the back-and-forth reveals

Not a chronicle this time. This section pulls specific prompts out of the curated log — moments where what Nolan said, and how he said it, tells you something about how he works: his taste, where his own professional background is doing the driving, when he leads the agent firmly instead of letting it wander into open-ended planning, and where he holds a line versus where he changes course. It is not a record of what shipped (see the archive links at the bottom of this page for that) and it deliberately skips PR numbers and setup mechanics — nobody reading this needs those to see the pattern.

A note on what this section actually is: everything below the quotes is Claude's reading of a small number of documented moments, not a settled account of Nolan's character. A single prompt can suggest a working style without proving one — these are inferences offered for Nolan to confirm, correct, or cut, the same way an unverified citation sits under review elsewhere in this tool. Treat the interpretation as a claim to check against the quote, not the other way around.

Leading, not being led

Agents left to their own devices tend to spiral into planning. These are moments where Nolan visibly refused to let that happen, or drew a line the agent itself hadn't drawn.

"here's the deal we dont need to expand claude md. just start making the html file first prototype. i believe in your capabilities"

Entry 3 · 2026-05-21

This is the whole session — one line, no back-and-forth, and it's the seed commit of what became v1. The pattern repeats throughout the log: when an agent might default to scaffolding and discussion instead of writing code, Nolan tends to cut straight to pointing it at the actual artifact.

Entry 28 · 2026-07-10, 18:25 → 18:38 — thirteen minutes, three prompts

18:25
"Let's have "Reading Mode" Swallow the "Turn Jiggle Off" aspect of the jiggle button. Right now, the flavor notes are all vibrating no matter waht. i need them to rest at equilibrium reasonably timely."
18:35
"Let's Make reading mode default. i like the ux of this"
18:38
"Too many options is my point. Let's remove these options entirely and make the state be the default experience of the app"

Three asks in a row, each one further than the last: merge two toggles into one, then make that one on by default, then realize a default anyone can still switch off is still a decision being asked of the user — so cut the option itself. Nobody suggested that last move; it's a preference for fewer decisions surfaced at all, arrived at faster than most single UI requests take to resolve.

"i think i also need an honesty policy in here where all citations have a 'under human review' until I personally review them and verify that they are indeed not hallucinated"

Entry 24 · 2026-07-01

Nobody asked for this — it surfaces unprompted, mid-session, as a standing rule rather than a reaction to a problem the agent found first. It's still the mechanism behind UNVERIFIED_REFS today, though its own wording later got revisited (see below).

Where the expertise shows

Moments where a specific professional instinct — not just an opinion — is visibly steering the decision.

Said directly, while asking the agent to write an About section grounded in his own information-design decisions rather than industry-standard tasting conventions — the one place in the log where the background driving the rest of this cluster is stated outright instead of inferred from a decision.

10:48

"argh when I try to organize the icons… the hweel jumbles all over again and it resets its somewhere else entirely… it's like bllowing up a balloon with hot air and all of the gas molecules are colliding with each other in the confined space."

Every correction here is a physical metaphor, not a UI spec — balloon, basil seeds, fluid in a petri dish, rave mode — and the fix that eventually landed (cached per-viewport home positions, a softened freeze curve, a manual freeze toggle) tracked those metaphors almost literally. It also took a real overcorrection in the middle: tuning for "massive widescreen monitor" produced something so fast on desktop it earned "rave mode activated," which then needed pulling back for both screens separately. The vision wasn't hit on the first pass, or the second — it took naming what was wrong six different ways before the physics actually matched what he meant.

The taste calls

Aesthetic and audience judgment, stated plainly and acted on immediately.

"most people dont know what blackcurrant tastes like here, so perhaps find other analogs that would be apparent to a wider audience."

Entry 4 · 2026-05-21

One line inside a longer feedback pass, but it set a durable rule still visible in later scope calls: a reference flavor only earns a place if the audience can actually recognize it. The correction isn't about the chemistry being wrong, it's about who the tool is legible to.

"keep in mind though wine will inspire this, i feel the tone of this is getting too wine specific. remember this needs to be accessible to a non-drinking or sober crowd most of all."

Entry 9 · 2026-05-26

Caught mid-edit, before the drift became load-bearing. The same prompt draws a careful line rather than a blanket ban — a few sentences later he explicitly carves out an exception for oak-barrel compounds, since those are genuinely interesting chemistry rather than wine-culture framing. That's a taste call with a boundary, not a blunt rule.

What he'll actually reconsider

The flip side of directiveness — moments of catching his own gap, or being visibly surprised into a new idea by something the tool showed him.

"argh, mobile ui didn't get the same attention. let's make sure the mobile portrait mode gets the same visual upgrades appropriate for the smaller screen size."

Entry 21 · 2026-06-30

This lands partway through a long, open-ended desktop UI brief, and the gap gets corrected inside the same session rather than becoming a later pass — a working style that self-audits mid-stream instead of needing an outside nudge to notice an oversight.

"that is not clear. it makes it seem like 'Lychee' is the 'real' smell of rose. that is pretty cool but is it accurate?"

Entry 44 · 2026-08-14

Before accepting a surprising result from his own tool, he checked it for accuracy first — the next prompt in the same exchange is "that's kind of neat if I start saying I smell 'lychee' when I'm smelling rose." The order matters: skepticism came before delight, not after. That sequence — verify, then let a genuinely new idea in — reads as a more careful kind of open-mindedness than simply being agreeable.

"the citation honesty policy does the opposite. it makes it sound dishonest and possibly completly false. remove this entire section and also the flags for under construction. once citations make it in the tool they have been checked because the literature itself is where we got the info in the first place. just say what criteria was considered for choosing works cited to rely on."

Entry 43 · 2026-08-04

A policy he set himself a month earlier (see entry 24, above) gets reversed here — not because the underlying practice changed, but because seeing it live in copy revealed it was undermining the very trust it was meant to build. Reworking his own earlier decision once he could see its actual effect is a different kind of receptiveness than being talked into something by the agent — this one came from evaluating his own past call against the shipped result.

How ideas evolved

The moments above show what individual prompts reveal about how Nolan works. This section does something narrower: it picks specific ideas that visibly changed over time and traces each one through the actual words that changed it, in order. Every quote below is pulled directly from the prompt log — either from the curated README's own quoted fragments, or, where noted, verified against the verbatim raw transcripts. Dates and entry numbers sit next to each one so the chain can be checked.

"Your perceptions are real, and they change"

The project's core stance, stated once, then tested, then lived out.

Entry 6 · 2026-05-23 → 05-26
"Yes the tone of the entire project is. Your perceptions are real, and they are going to change depending on what exact moment you use the tool. It's gustatory agnostic…"
The stance is stated flatly, as a direct answer to a question about tone — and immediately scoped: gustatory-agnostic in principle, but edible-only in practice for now.
Entry 8 · 2026-05-23
"Let's really think about if this is the right stance? : ('here's the chemistry your perception implies')…"
Days later, the same idea gets re-litigated rather than assumed — the stance survives its own stress test, reframed as "here's the chemistry your perception implies" instead of a claim about ground truth.
Entry 44 · 2026-08-14
"that's kind of neat if I start saying I smell 'lychee' when I'm smelling rose"
Three months later, the abstract stance shows up as a lived reaction to the tool's own output — scoring a rose tasting and finding "Strongest: Lychee," and treating that as an invitation rather than a correction.

The citation honesty rule

Pushed for, then built as an explicit policy, then rewritten because the policy's own wording undermined it.

Entry 15 · 2026-06-09 → 06-11
"are there not citations we can get from within the last decade?"
The earliest push is simple: are these sources actually current. That question is what starts the project interrogating its own citations rather than just adding them.
Entry 24 · 2026-07-01
"i think i also need an honesty policy in here…" (full quote above, in "Leading, not being led")
That becomes an explicit rule (50996dd): unverified sources render as under review rather than getting silently trusted. This is still the mechanism behind UNVERIFIED_REFS today.
Entry 43 · 2026-08-04 → 08-05
"it makes it sound dishonest and possibly completely false…" (full quote above, in "What he'll actually reconsider")
A month later, the underlying practice doesn't change — but the copy describing it does (850afa1). The honesty policy had started reading as an admission of doubt instead of a description of a process, and got rewritten to state the actual selection criteria instead.

The mobile-first pivot

From "test it at phone size" to a documented product-design contract.

Entry 19 · 2026-06-27 → 06-28
"…because that's honestly how it will be demoed irl"
The Galaxy S10e portrait viewport becomes the working reference size for a very practical reason — that's the actual demo conditions — not an abstract accessibility ideal.
Entry 21 · 2026-06-29 → 07-01
"argh, mobile ui didn't get the same attention"
A long open-ended desktop UI brief gets self-corrected mid-session once the gap becomes visible — mobile gets its own matching treatment (natural page scroll, a fixed Tasting Tray) in the same session rather than a later pass.
Entry 35 · 2026-07-25 → 07-26
"i know overall i was working on ui design for both desktop and mobile versions and trying to figure out how to clean up the overall transitions between different features… so that it is more seamless when someone is in the middle of a tasting session…"
What had been recurring in-session corrections finally gets written down as a standing rule: the mobile-first product-design contract now in CLAUDE.md (17c7fdd) — named viewport classes, no critical control may clip, pane ownership belongs to the user.

The Tree's taxonomy: tags to a real tree to a spiral to leaves

The longest-running visual-design thread in the project.

Entry 20 · 2026-06-28 → 06-29
"can we put all of the flavor notes on a taxonomy tree? this is the third viewer along with wheel and map"
A Phase 1 family-tag layer (added earlier the same session) becomes a full Kingdom→Order→Family→Note dendrogram — a real phylogenetic depth, not just a label on a note.
Entry 37 · 2026-07-26
"might do better if when you zoom to family level it looks more like a wreath… to carry over the circle design of aroma wheel"
A design idea relayed from a conversation with ChatGPT reframes the family level visually, tying the Tree's shape back to the Wheel's.
Entry 40 · 2026-07-30
"desktop view is still pretty unviewable because its a super side but short interactive… is there another way to show hierarchy while spiraling?"
A purely visual pass turns out not to use the desktop screen well; naming that problem directly produces the conical-helix layout (61d069f).
Entry 38 · 2026-07-26
"yeah maybe it should read as leaves at that point yes you go for it with your ui artistry"
The family scale gets read as leaves rather than branches (060519e), then as growth rings (1a557ee) — the wreath idea from entry 37 finding its literal shape.

ChatGPT and the codex branch: quarantine to trust

From containment to an explicit reversal of Claude's own caution.

Entry 29 · 2026-07-11
"in case any new openai models hopefully will keep whatever bugs or snips inside its own area and we keep most of the repo supported solely by claudel"
The codex/* policy starts as containment, stated plainly as such — not a judgment about ChatGPT's work quality, a fence around risk.
Entry 34 · 2026-07-11
"update the rule where you can also update this branch. this is just the only one i want chatgpt touching"
Claude's access to the codex branch, previously a one-off exception, becomes a standing rule — the fence stays, but it stops being one-directional.
Entry 39 · 2026-07-27
"that's against the branching rule in CLAUDE.md… I trust codex to do everything claude can do now that Ive had time to test their newest model. its trustworthy."
The extraction/re-implementation gate — a rule Claude had cited back defensively — gets explicitly overridden. codex/* can merge directly into prototype; Claude's review moves from a pre-merge gate to periodic post-hoc review, as it still is today.

Go Deeper's ortho/retro mechanism

From a data-logging toggle to a framing for a whole audience.

Entry 6 · 2026-05-23 → 05-26
"So yes being able to click either ortho (sniff) or retro (swallow) and that option should always be available for the user to flag for themselves. and that can be another visibility toggle in the legend."
Ortho/retro starts as an always-visible flag on every note, sitting in the legend alongside other visibility toggles.
Entry 6 · 2026-05-23 → 05-26
"ortho nasal is fine behind an advanced frame"
Within the same session, the mechanism gets pulled back behind an advanced frame — the first move toward what becomes Go Deeper.
Entry 6 · 2026-05-23 → 05-26
"every taste note on the left pane can have ortho/retro on there and that is what confirms the note's presence on the central wheel and in the analysis. to confirm you need to select ortho(sniff) or retro(Swallow)."
It generalizes further — from an optional flag to the actual confirmation mechanism for entering a note at all, with a checkmark indicator so a user knows what they've already logged.
Entry 42 · 2026-08-06 → 08-07
"im really trying to key into the people that will use go deeper because they are inquisitive and want to go deeper into how these notes relate and how to detect these different compounds by inferring from detecting flavors/aromas with our senses."
Months later, Go Deeper stops being framed as a data-input mechanism at all and gets explicitly redefined around an audience — people curious about relationships between notes, not just a stricter logging mode. Map and Tree get ungated from behind it the same session.

Two threads worth naming were considered and dropped. The "no invented distinguishing compound" pattern (noStandoutNote) is real and consistently applied — but the log only carries one clean verbatim quote for it (entry 19's berry table framing), not enough steps to call an evolution rather than a single decision. The economic-cultivation and cultural-value map layers mentioned in CLAUDE.md are explicitly still unstarted, pending design approval, so there's no mutation to trace yet.
