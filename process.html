The Human Record

What in this app is actually mine, and what came from Claude — with the receipts, not just the claim.

Drafted by Claude, not yet reviewed by Nolan — treat this page as a first pass, the same way an unverified citation sits under review elsewhere in this tool

The question

Aroma Wheel's code was written by an AI. Every line of JavaScript, every CSS rule, the whole knowledge graph's plumbing — none of it was typed character-by-character by a person. That's true and worth saying plainly, not softened. But "an AI made this" undersells what actually happened, and "I made this" overclaims it. This page is an attempt to be honest about the difference, in both directions.

Two different things "who made this" can mean

If the question is "whose hands typed the characters," the answer is almost entirely Claude's. The literal syntax of a D3 force simulation, the shape of a CSS grid, the phrasing conventions of a methodology writeup — that fluency comes from a training process built on an enormous amount of other people's code, prose, and published research, compressed into a model. None of that is Nolan's authorship, and this page won't pretend otherwise.

If the question is "whose decisions shaped what exists," the answer runs the other way. What compound gets cited and what gets rejected as unverifiable. Whether the wheel splits notes from taste-alikes. Why blackcurrant got swapped for something more people have actually tasted. Why the tool refuses to grade you against a "correct" answer. Why an old 1981 threshold paper outranks a newer one that just cites it. Whether a varietal note is honest about having "no standout compound" instead of inventing one to fill a table cell. None of that came from Claude's training data on its own — it came from several months of Nolan pushing, correcting, and re-scoping, one prompt at a time, documented in the prompt log linked below.

Those two answers don't average into one tidy percentage, and this page isn't going to invent one. A single number would flatten a distinction that actually matters: authorship of the text and authorship of the judgment are different kinds of contribution, and collapsing them into "70% human" or "30% human" would be a more confident-sounding claim than the truth supports.

What can actually be checked

Instead of a percentage, here's what's verifiable. This project keeps a log of every prompt that went into it — not a summary written after the fact, but the actual words, extracted from session transcripts, published alongside the sessions where the transcript no longer exists to double-check it. The same honesty rule that governs this tool's citations (real source or it says so, no invented-sounding plausible filler) governs this log too.

Why this page exists at all

This tool has said elsewhere that its point isn't just the finished interface, it's the thinking that shaped it. That claim is empty if the thinking stays invisible. This page, and the log it points to, exist so that claim has to answer to something — and so the honest, less flattering half of the answer (that the actual words in this app aren't Nolan's) sits right next to the half that's easy to be proud of (that very little of what those words do was left to the model to decide alone).
