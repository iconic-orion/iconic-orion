00.5 / This way of working predates AI

The unofficial route is part of the method.

The Aroma Wheel is not the first time Nolan has used a tool outside a conventional job title or credential path to make a difficult system operable. The recurring skill is not knowing every formal procedure in advance. It is finding the structure, building a usable model, and checking whether the model still makes sense when it touches reality.

“I feel that I do a lot of approximations to the ‘official’ way to do something.”
Nolan · 2026-08-18 · current conversation
Nolan's account, condensed

A GIS problem hiding inside months of manual work.

Just out of college, Nolan helped a maternal-health research lab approximate neighborhood street connectivity for more than 300 participants. The proposed hand process involved counting intersections around each home. He brought his own GIS access and translated the task into a repeatable spatial workflow.

01 / Encounter
Find the live bottleneck.A meaningful research question is blocked by a process too slow to perform consistently by hand.
02 / Translate
Turn procedure into geometry.Homes become points, ten-mile study areas become circles, and intersections become countable spatial features.
03 / Automate
Make one method repeatable.A GIS workflow replaces hundreds of separate manual counts and preserves one consistent rule across participants.
04 / Defend
Interrogate the surprising number.When counts reached into the thousands, Nolan used tables and neighborhood maps to show why dense suburban and rural street patterns produced very different results.
05 / Rescope
Let the result revise the question.The output exposed overlap and scale: a ten-mile radius could be too broad when many participants shared neighborhoods across one region.

That does not substitute for formal domain credentials where credentials are required. It does explain why conversation-to-code is useful here: Nolan already thinks in interacting systems and uses the agent to externalize them into a medium other people can enter.

00.75 / Facilitation is experience authorship

Design the conditions. Let someone else make the discovery.

For nearly four years, Nolan practiced translation in public: live science shows, weekly K–8 rooms, scientist engagement, institutional meetings, student cohorts, and professional-development workshops. The job was not to stand between people and the science. It was to build the sequence through which they could reach one another.

A narrative made from a box, a sound, permission, and a reveal.

The activity does not begin by explaining inference. It creates a need to infer, then gives the group language for what they just did.

01 / Place
Objects arrive before instructions.The facilitator can observe who assumes they may touch, who waits for permission, and how institutional habits shape participation before the lesson is named.
02 / Withhold
The box stays closed.A ball chain may sound like rolling or skidding. A sealed water bottle, hinge, rattle, or clink can suggest a mechanism far more elaborate than the object inside.
03 / Investigate
Participants make evidence.They tilt, shake, listen, compare, model an interior, disagree, and decide what another test might reveal.
04 / Reveal
The ordinary object creates surprise.Opening the box does not merely announce a right answer. It exposes the distance between evidence, inference, imagination, and the mechanism that was actually there.
05 / Return
Now reveal the why.Why were the objects placed out early? Why did some people reach immediately while others waited? What did each test support, and what did the evidence never prove?

Inherited form, named rather than claimedNolan learned this mystery-box practice through a colleague who had worked at Explora in Albuquerque. Explora's official record documents a Mystery Boxes Teacher Workshop centered on curiosity, problem-solving, inquiry, and inference. That supports the institutional connection. It does not identify the activity's original creator or prove that Nolan's exact box design originated there.

Interpretation boundary: teachers waiting for permission while scientists reached for the objects is Nolan's observation from facilitated sessions, not a universal behavioral claim about either group. Its value here is as an example of how an experience can make tacit norms visible.

“stories, narrative, it's not just prose in general that reaches for the heart, but the rhythm in narrative that humans are so good at doing.” Set the scene → withhold just enough → invite action → let tension accumulate → reveal → return to the why

01 / The control surface

Conversation did not replace direction. It became the way direction reached the code.

An agent can hold the implementation tools while the human steers through language, perception, refusal, and revision. The useful unit is not “the prompt.” It is the loop.

01 / Articulate

Name the desired experience.

Often as a sensation, spatial relationship, story, or frustration before it is a technical requirement.

02 / Translate

Agent renders a hypothesis.

Code, copy, and interface structure make one possible reading of the direction visible.

03 / Perceive

Inspect what feels wrong.

The artifact reveals assumptions that were impossible to correct while they were still abstract.

04 / Correct

Put the distance into words.

The next prompt is not “more.” It narrows, reverses, removes, or gives the agent room in a specific place.

05 / Accrete

Let decisions become structure.

Repeated corrections become defaults, policies, data models, and visual grammar.

What that shorthand gets wrong

“The AI wrote the code” is materially true. “Therefore the AI made the work” only follows if making is defined as typing syntax and nothing else.

The disputed territory is not who typed. It is whether originating the system, directing its behavior, testing it in lived use, and repeatedly deciding what the tool should become count as authorship.

This record cannot force a peer to use a broader definition. It can make the narrower definition visible, so the disagreement is about authorship itself rather than a mistaken story that one prompt produced the app.

Nolan: the live purpose

The app is the teaching surface. Nolan leads the tasting.

“this is a teaching aid while i lead others into their first tasting experiences with more depth than ‘this tastes good, this doesn't’ to ‘is that a terpene or an ester i'm detecting?’”

01 / Preference
“Do I like it?”A valid beginning, but not the end of what a person can notice.
02 / Perception
“What changed?”Slow down enough to notice aroma, timing, texture, and the difference between sniffing and swallowing.
03 / Language
“What does it remind me of?”Turn private sensation into shareable words without grading the perception as right or wrong.
04 / Relationship
“Where else does this occur?”Compare notes through plant family, geography, and shared aroma compounds.
05 / Inquiry
“Terpene or ester?”Use the evidence layer to form a chemistry-informed question and decide where to go deeper.
The interface supplies structure; the facilitator supplies attention. The wheel can surface plausible chemical relationships, but a sensory description by itself does not identify a molecule or chemical class with laboratory certainty. The educational move is from unexamined preference toward a better-supported question, not from first taste to false confirmation.

04 / Where the line actually is

The archive can support a claim without turning every interpretation into fact.

Consensus starts to diverge when documented events become claims about interior authorship, creative ownership, or the model's training lineage. The page should let a reader see exactly where that transition happens.

Observable

  • Verbatim human prompts survive in the archive.
  • Git shows which artifacts and behaviors changed afterward.
  • The implementation was overwhelmingly typed by agents.
  • Repeated human corrections shaped defaults, scope, and visual behavior.

Interpreted

  • What one prompt reveals about Nolan's working style.
  • Whether a correction demonstrates expertise, taste, caution, or all three.
  • Where one idea ends and a jointly developed idea begins.
  • The narrative groupings used on this page.

Not supportable

  • A clean human-versus-agent percentage.
  • A claim that the agent contributed no creative judgment.
  • A claim that Nolan directly authored agent-written syntax.
  • Per-output attribution to particular people whose work influenced model training.
