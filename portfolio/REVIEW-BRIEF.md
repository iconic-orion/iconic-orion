# Review brief: representing Nolan's human narrative

This is a durable handoff for Nolan, Claude, Codex, other agents, and human reviewers. It records what this portfolio experiment is trying to represent, where the current visual ideas came from, and what kind of review would be useful.

The pages in this directory are not part of the deployed Aroma Wheel site. They remain experimental work on `codex/human-authorship-story` until Nolan explicitly decides how they should relate to the public project.

## The human narrative we are trying to represent

The central story is not that Nolan supplied a prompt and an agent supplied a finished product. Nolan brings a body of lived sensory perception, science communication, facilitation, systems reasoning, composition, simulation, community knowledge, private significance, and repeated judgment. A prompt compresses that material into language. An agent turns the language into a visible hypothesis. Nolan then uses recognition, expertise, discomfort, and taste to inspect the distance between the hypothesis and what he meant.

The resulting artifact is built through repeated movement across that distance.

The page should make three things perceptible at the same time:

1. Nolan's thinking and direction precede the code.
2. Agents contribute real synthesis and implementation rather than functioning as neutral pencils.
3. Those contributions are not interchangeable with Nolan's lived experience, cultural situation, decision authority, or final adoption.

## Provenance of the current direction

### Nolan: direct direction and language

Nolan asked for:

- more vibrant color;
- a more narratively driven page centered on “The Agent & The Human”;
- a page that morphs or melts into a liminal space;
- raw prompts lined up sequentially and scanning through the background without all being visible simultaneously;
- the feeling that something beneath the surface is trying to grasp what he is laying down in prose;
- recognition that personally significant and apparently arbitrary choices may carry community-specific feeling or in-humor;
- the destination: “true coexistance requires me to integrate shared reality in shared space.”

Those phrases and the exact Nolan quotations used in the page should remain visibly attributed to him. Spelling and grammar inside exact quotations should not be silently normalized.

### Codex: current interpretation

Codex translated that direction into:

- a six-act narrative: arrival, compression, scanning, mismatch, local meaning, and shared reality;
- saturated orange/magenta for Nolan's lived signal, cyan/blue for agent synthesis, violet for the liminal membrane, and acid green for shared/adopted reality;
- a moving, numbered substrate made from excerpts of Nolan's prompts;
- an explicit translation boundary distinguishing the metaphor of an agent “trying to grasp” from claims of consciousness or lived understanding;
- a four-beat loop: Nolan lays down the signal, the agent renders a hypothesis, Nolan inspects the distance, and the shared artifact is revised;
- a final move away from a competitive human-versus-agent binary toward asymmetrical coexistence in a shared, inspectable object.

These structures and connective sentences are agent synthesis. Nolan has not automatically endorsed every sentence merely because it appears in the draft.

### Claude or another reviewer: contribution still open

No later reviewer should be credited with ideas they have not yet supplied. A review, proposed change, and Nolan-approved adoption are three different provenance events and should remain distinguishable.

## Where Claude's eye may help

Claude's prior UI work often has a recognizable editorial flavor: quieter typographic pacing, carefully bounded translucent surfaces, atmospheric transitions, and an ability to make documentation feel curated. That eye may help with the following questions:

1. Does the moving prompt substrate feel like sequential thought passing beneath the page, or merely like decorative code rain?
2. Can the transition from defined human/agent colors into the shared violet-green field feel like an actual spatial threshold rather than a gradient swap?
3. Does each act reveal one new piece of the narrative, or does the page explain its thesis too early and then repeat it?
4. Can the page preserve its new vibrancy without losing the legibility and restraint that made Iteration 01 feel evidentiary?
5. Are there places where agent commentary only paraphrases an adjacent Nolan quote and should therefore be removed?
6. Does “The Agent & The Human” read as an asymmetrical collaboration with clear boundaries, or accidentally imply equal consciousness, experience, or authority?
7. On a compact phone, does the prompt scan remain atmosphere while the human narrative remains the foreground?

## Non-negotiable boundaries for review

- Preserve Iteration 01. Iteration 02 is a parallel interpretation, not a replacement.
- Keep Nolan's exact language visibly labeled and separate from paraphrase.
- Do not infer conceptual authorship from Git commit authorship.
- Do not imply that an agent has lived experience, cultural membership, private feeling, or consciousness.
- Do not reduce the narrative to “Nolan had ideas; AI typed code.” The translation, mismatch, correction, and adoption loop is the subject.
- Do not turn the page into a generic AI-transparency explainer. It is specifically about Nolan's way of sensing, teaching, modeling, communicating, and directing.
- Do not link these drafts into the Aroma Wheel website or merge them into `prototype` without Nolan's explicit approval.

## Requested review format

For every substantial suggestion, label it as one of:

- **Preserve** — something already carrying the intended narrative clearly.
- **Propose** — a reviewer-authored alternative not yet adopted by Nolan.
- **Question** — an ambiguity that requires Nolan's lived knowledge or preference.
- **Boundary risk** — a place where provenance, anthropomorphism, cultural meaning, accessibility, or public/private scope may be misread.

Reference the affected act, quotation, or visual behavior. Explain what the proposed change would make a reader feel or understand differently. Do not describe a visual change only as “cleaner,” “better,” or “more polished.”
